# What hardware fits the realism-graded simulation

Every number in the left-hand columns below is **read from the resolved configuration**, not
retyped. Regenerate the whole set with:

```
matlab -batch "addpath('analysis'); addpath('config'); report_hardware_requirements('golden_baseline_attitude.json')"
```

`analysis/report_hardware_requirements.m` resolves any scenario JSON through the canonical
pipeline (`masterConfig` then `realismGradeConfig` then the `_extends` chain) and re-expresses
each sigma in the units a datasheet quotes. Unit conversion is the only arithmetic it performs,
so a printed requirement cannot drift from what the simulation actually ran.

**Provenance of the right-hand columns.** Simulation values and every unit conversion are
generated by the script and are exact. Vendor figures were taken from datasheets and vendor
pages in August 2026 and are quoted at the level of "does this class of part clear the
requirement". Verified directly against the source document: Sensonor STIM377H (0.15 deg/sqrt(h)
ARW, 0.3 deg/h bias instability, 55 g, 1.5 W), Microchip SA.45s Space CSAC (3.0e-10 at 1 s,
about 3e-10/month drift beyond 1e4 s), Safran RAFS (about 2e-14 at 1e4 s, aging below
5e-14/day). Pull the current datasheet before citing any specific part number in the thesis.

**Which config you resolve matters.** Three values differ between the bare `realism.json`
profile and the goldens that inherit it:

| Path | `realism.json` alone | all three goldens |
|---|---|---|
| `errors.hardwareDelay.sigma_m` | 0.50 m (1.67 ns) | 0.05 m (0.167 ns) |
| `errors.interAntennaCarrierBias.sigma_cycles` | 0.25 (47.6 mm) | 0.02 in `_attitude`, 0.25 in the other two |
| `multiAsset.twoWayISL.sigma_m` | 0.01 m (33.4 ps) | 0.05 m (167 ps) in `_multi` |

Quote the golden you actually ran. The tables below use
`golden_baseline_attitude.json` unless stated.

## 1. Spacecraft clock

Requirement: `CESIUM1` from the Winkel (2003) Table 2.1 catalogue, h0 = 1e-19,
h-1 = 1e-25, h-2 = 2e-32.

| tau | 1 s | 10 s | 100 s | 1000 s | 1 h | 4 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sigma_y(tau) | 2.2e-10 | 7.1e-11 | 2.2e-11 | 7.1e-12 | 3.7e-12 | 1.9e-12 |

| Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Space RAFS** (Safran/Orolia, Excelitas) | **Fits with ~100x margin.** Safran quotes about 2e-14 at 1e4 s and aging below 5e-14/day. This is the defensible pick. |
| **Microchip SA.45s Space CSAC** | **Marginal, do not claim it.** Spec is 3.0e-10 at 1 s, already worse than the 2.2e-10 the simulation assumes, and beyond 1e4 s it is dominated by about 3e-10/month physics-package drift. It does not clear 1.9e-12 at 4 h with margin. |
| Space USO / OCXO | Not comparable as modelled. `driftRate_fracPerSec = 0` and h1 = h2 = 0, so **no aging term is simulated at all**. A crystal's real 4-hour limit is aging, which this model does not charge. Comparing a USO against this curve flatters it. |

The short-tau end is the surprise: h0 = 1e-19 gives 2.2e-10 at 1 s, which a modern RAFS beats by
roughly 70x. The simulated on-board clock is a 2003 textbook caesium beam, not current hardware.

## 2. Ground clock and its broadcast product

| Quantity | Value | Datasheet form |
|---|---|---|
| `towers(k).clockType` | `OCXO`, which resolves to catalogue **OCXO2** | label only, see below |
| `towers(k).clock.noiseCoeffs` | h0 = 2.51e-26, h-1 = 2.51e-23, h-2 = 2.51e-22 | sigma_y: **4.11e-11 at 1 s, 4.06e-10 at 100 s, 2.44e-9 at 1 h, 4.88e-9 at 4 h** |
| `towers(k).clock.deterministic` | `false` on all five | oscillator noise is LIVE, not inert |
| `clocks.tower.product.sigmaBias_m` | 0.10 m | **0.334 ns** time-transfer product accuracy |
| `clocks.tower.product.sigmaDrift_mps` | 0.001 m/s | **3.34 ps/s** frequency product accuracy |

**Read `clockType` as a label, not as a specification.** `masterConfig.m:1946` writes `'OCXO'`,
and `ConfigFactory.getClockTemplate_` maps that alias to **OCXO2**, not OCXO1. The two entries
are not neighbours: OCXO2 has the best short-term stability in the whole catalogue and the worst
long-term drift. Quote `clock.noiseCoeffs` off the resolved config, which is the table
`ClockModel` actually integrates. (Earlier revisions of this section quoted the OCXO1 curve,
2.1e-10 at 1 s and 1.9e-9 at 4 h, because `report_hardware_requirements.m` kept its own alias
table that mapped `OCXO` to the wrong entry. Fixed 2026-08-14: the script now reads
`noiseCoeffs` directly and cannot drift again.)

Two things to say about this, and they point in opposite directions.

The **oscillator** is modelled worse than reality, and increasingly so with averaging time. A
real timing-station OCXO sits near 1e-12 at 1 s, about 41x better than the OCXO2 entry. At long
tau the gap widens: OCXO2 is random-walk dominated (h-2 = 2.51e-22), so it degrades to 4.88e-9
at 4 h rather than levelling off. That is deliberate conservatism, not a procurement target.

The long-tau penalty is also largely notional, because the ground oscillator never runs open
loop that long. `clocks.tower.product.updateInterval_s = 30` with `latency_s = 5` re-anchors it
about every 35 s, where OCXO2 sits at 2.4e-10. The tail of that curve is not a requirement any
real timing station would have to meet.

The **product accuracy** is the binding spec and it is realistic: 0.334 ns is IGS-RTS real-time
grade. Delivering it at each of five sites means a disciplined reference (caesium or active
hydrogen maser) plus a geodetic timing receiver, or a two-way link. This is rack equipment, so
it sits outside the "COTS space-graded" scope of the question, but it is worth naming because
the project's own finding is that **the ground clock dominates and the space clock is close to
irrelevant**. The most consequential clock requirement in this simulation is not a spacecraft
component at all.

## 3. Star tracker

| Quantity | Value | Datasheet form |
|---|---|---|
| `whiteAngularSigma_rad` | 1.45444e-4 | **30 arcsec 1-sigma, per axis** |
| `updatePeriod_s` | 1 | 1 Hz quaternion output |
| `truth.alignmentDriftRandomWalk` | 0 | perfect body alignment assumed |

The simulation applies an **isotropic** 30 arcsec. Real trackers are 5 to 10x worse about the
boresight than across it, so the binding number is the **roll** spec, not the headline
cross-boresight one.

| Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Ball CT-2020** | **Fits on all three axes.** Below 1.5 arcsec cross-boresight, below 15 arcsec roll to end of life. |
| Sodern Auriga / Hydra, Jena-Optronik ASTRO APS | Fit comfortably. Hydra's multi-head arrangement removes the roll weakness by construction. |
| **Small NST-class trackers** (around 50 arcsec) | **Fail on roll.** A unit quoted at 6 arcsec cross-boresight and 40 arcsec roll does not meet an isotropic 30 arcsec. |

Safest architectural answer: **two heads**, which makes the roll axis as good as the cross axes
and removes the single-axis argument entirely.

**Gap no purchase can close.** `fixedAlignmentBias_rad` and `alignmentDriftRandomWalk` are both
zero, so the tracker-to-body alignment is perfect and non-drifting. Real thermoelastic alignment
drift on a GEO bus is the same order as the 30 arcsec noise itself. This is a structures and
thermal requirement, and the simulation currently does not carry it.

## 4. Gyro

| Quantity | Value | Datasheet form |
|---|---|---|
| `imu.truth.arw_rad_per_sqrt_s` | 2e-4 | **0.688 deg/sqrt(h)** angle random walk |
| `imu.truth.rrw_rad_per_s_sqrt_s` | 3e-6 | **37.1 deg/h/sqrt(h)** rate random walk |
| `imu.truth.bias0Sigma_radps` | 3e-5 | **6.19 deg/h** turn-on bias 1-sigma |

| Candidate | ARW | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensonor STIM377H** (COTS, hermetic, LEO-optimised, 55 g, 1.5 W) | 0.15 deg/sqrt(h), 0.3 deg/h bias instability | **Fits with 4.6x margin.** |
| Northrop Grumman LN-200S | about 0.05 deg/sqrt(h) | Fits, 14x margin |
| Safran Astrix, Honeywell MIMU, NG SiRU | 1e-4 to 1e-3 deg/sqrt(h) | Fit by two to three orders |

**The gyro is not a design driver in this simulation.** The requirement is so loose that every
space-grade COTS IMU clears it, and the simulated rate random walk (bias wandering 37 deg/h over
one hour) is two orders worse than a MEMS unit's 0.3 deg/h bias-instability floor. Any attitude
result from these runs is therefore a **lower bound** on what real hardware delivers.

One caveat if you cite STIM377H: it is qualified and optimised for LEO. A long GEO mission has a
different total-dose and lifetime case, which pushes toward the Astrix / SiRU / MIMU class.

## 5. Payload receiver (space segment)

| Quantity | Value | Datasheet form |
|---|---|---|
| `signals.L1` / `L2` | 1575.42 / 1227.60 MHz | lambda 190.3 / 244.2 mm |
| `codeNoise.cn0.base_dBHz` | 45 | 45 dB-Hz nominal, +6 dB at zenith |
| `codeNoise.cn0.sigmaAt45dBHz_m` | 0.30 m | **1.0 ns DLL jitter** |
| `codeNoise.cn0.minTrackable_dBHz` | 25 | acquisition threshold |
| `measurements.carrier.sigma_m` | 0.010 m | **10 mm, 0.053 cycles of L1** |
| `measurements.doppler.sigma_mps` | 0.0424 m/s | **0.223 Hz FLL jitter at L1** |
| `asset.receiverLeverArms_body_m` | 4 antennas | **one oscillator, four phase centres** |

On noise alone this is a comfortable spec. A POD-class dual-frequency space receiver
(Beyond Gravity **PODRIX**, flown on Sentinel-6A for few-centimetre POD, or the ESA GAMIR-2
class) achieves 1 to 2 mm carrier phase noise, so the 10 mm realism floor is 5 to 10x
conservative, and 0.30 m code is loose.

**The noise is not the hard part. The architecture is.** Two things put this outside a catalogue
purchase:

1. **Four phase centres on a common clock.** Space GNSS receivers are typically one or two
   antennas. Four-antenna common-oscillator space receivers are not a shelf item.
2. **The signals come from ground towers, not from GNSS satellites.** This is reverse GNSS. The
   front end is the same L-band, but no COTS GNSS receiver tracks a ground-transmitted PN code
   out of the box.

The realistic build is a POD-class front end retargeted, or a space SDR (Innoflight, Syrlinks,
Tethers Unlimited class) carrying a GNSS-style correlator. This is the one component where
"COTS space-graded" is a partial answer at best, and the thesis should say so rather than name a
part number.

## 6. RF chain and antenna array

| Quantity | Value | Datasheet form |
|---|---|---|
| `errors.hardwareDelay.sigma_m` | 0.05 m | **0.167 ns uncalibrated group delay, per tower** |
| `hardwareDelay.residualStochastic.enable` | true | the residual **drifts**, it is not a constant to calibrate out |
| `biases.interFrequency.code.truth.L1_m` / `L2_m` | 0.30 / 0.45 m | 1.0 / 1.5 ns differential code bias |
| baselines | 1.47 m adjacent, 2.00 m opposite | cross pattern `[1 -1 0 0; 0 0 1 -1; 0.2 0.2 -0.2 -0.2]` |
| `effects.antennaPCV.amplitude_m` | 0.005 m | **5 mm uncalibrated phase-centre variation** |
| `interAntennaCarrierBias.sigma_cycles` | 0.02 (attitude) / 0.25 (others) | **3.81 mm / 47.6 mm** inter-antenna phase-path mismatch at L1 |

Dual-band L1/L2 patch or helix antennas are available space-graded from Antcom, Haigh-Farr,
Anywaves and Beyond Gravity. But three of these rows are **build and calibration requirements,
not purchases**:

- **5 mm PCV** is achievable only with an ANTEX-style chamber calibration. A bare spacecraft
  patch runs 5 to 20 mm over the hemisphere.
- **3.81 mm inter-antenna residual** (the calibrated attitude case) implies a matched, calibrated
  four-channel RF harness with per-channel line-bias characterisation.
- **167 ps of drifting differential group delay** is a thermal-design number. It needs a
  temperature-stabilised front end and phase-stable cable (Gore PHASEFLEX or Times Microwave
  PhaseTrack class), and the fact that it drifts means it cannot be removed by a one-time
  ground calibration.

**The structure is the unmodelled driver.** At a 2.00 m baseline, 1 mm of thermoelastic motion is
0.5 mrad, i.e. 0.029 deg of apparent attitude. Sub-millimetre CFRP stability over the arc is the
real attitude requirement, and the simulation carries **no** thermoelastic baseline term. Note
also that **phase wind-up is not modelled anywhere** (stated explicitly in
`config/ladder/att/att001_*.json`), which matters most in exactly the configuration where
carrier phase is the sole source of absolute attitude.

## 7. Crosslink terminal

This is the section where reading the code changes the answer, so take it in order.

### 7a. EIRP and G/T are inert on the branch the goldens use

`golden_baseline_multi.json` runs `multiAsset.twoWayISL` with `linkBudget.model = 'linkBudget'`.
`revgnss.ISLLinkBudget.cn0Delta_dB` computes `-20*log10(d/d_ref)` and **nothing else**.
`EIRP_dBW = 15` and `GT_dBK = 5` are read into the struct and never used. So the crosslink sigma
in the swarm runs is an asserted number scaled by distance, and **no terminal specification can
be derived from those runs**.

### 7b. On the physicalRF branch, the antenna fields cancel exactly

`realismGradeConfig` sets `measurements.isl.twoWay.range.linkBudget.model = 'physicalRF'`, which
does run a real budget (though `range.enable` is false in the goldens). Two things there are
worth knowing before quoting any of it:

- `gain_dBi = 12` is **ignored**. With `antennaModel = 'fixedAperture'`,
  `InterSatelliteRFLinkModel` derives the gain from diameter and efficiency. At 26 GHz, 0.25 m,
  62 % that is **34.6 dBi**, beamwidth about 3.2 deg, not 12 dBi.
- `systemNoiseTemperature_K = 500` is also **ignored**, because
  `receiverNoiseDefinition = 'GT'`. The implied system temperature is 910 K.

And with `powerDefinition = 'eirp'` plus `receiverNoiseDefinition = 'GT'`, the receive gain
cancels algebraically:

```
C/N0 = EIRP + Grx - FSPL - losses + 228.6 - (Grx - GT)
     = EIRP + GT - FSPL - losses + 228.6
```

So the antenna geometry does not affect the result at all. **Only EIRP and G/T are real specs.**
At 1 km and 26 GHz this gives C/N0 = 124.9 dB-Hz against a 25 dB-Hz threshold, i.e. about
**100 dB of margin**, and an implied transmit power of 11 mW.

### 7c. What terminal actually satisfies EIRP 15 dBW and G/T 5 dB/K

| Aperture | Gain | Implied Tsys for G/T = 5 dB/K | Beamwidth | RF power for 15 dBW EIRP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 m | 20.6 dBi | 36 K | 16.1 deg | 275 mW |
| 0.075 m | 24.1 dBi | 82 K | 10.8 deg | 122 mW |
| **0.10 m** | **26.6 dBi** | **146 K** | **8.1 deg** | **69 mW** |
| 0.15 m | 30.2 dBi | 327 K | 5.4 deg | 31 mW |
| 0.25 m (as configured) | 34.6 dBi | 910 K | 3.2 deg | 11 mW |

A **10 cm aperture with a low-noise front end (Tsys around 150 K) and under 100 mW of RF**
satisfies both specs, with a manageable 8 deg beam. The configured 0.25 m dish is unnecessary and
its 3.2 deg beam imposes a pointing requirement the link does not need.

### 7d. The requirement that is actually hard

| Quantity | `_multi` | `realism.json` | Datasheet form |
|---|---|---|---|
| `twoWayISL.sigma_m` | 0.05 m | 0.01 m | 167 ps / 33.4 ps two-way thermal |
| `delayCal.sigma_const_m` | 0.005 m | 0.01 m | **16.7 ps / 33.4 ps absolute delay calibration** |
| `delayCal.sigma_rw_m` (tau = 3600 s) | 0.002 m | 0.003 m | **6.7 ps / 10 ps delay stability per hour** |

The RF link has 100 dB of margin. The binding requirement is **turn-around delay calibration at
16.7 ps absolute and 6.7 ps of drift per hour**. This is consistent with the project's own
measured result that formation shape is floored by delay calibration and not by ranging noise.

Equipment consequence, stated plainly: a COTS Ka SDR (Tethers Unlimited SWIFT-KTX, Orbital
Astronautics K/Ka, Syrlinks, Kongsberg) closes this link trivially but gives you **no
picosecond-class delay metrology**. Reaching 16.7 ps needs an internal calibration loop and a
coherent transponder architecture in the style of the GRACE-FO K-band ranging instrument or ESA
formation-flying RF metrology. **There is no drop-in COTS answer at that number.**

## 8. Summary

| Subsystem | Requirement | COTS space-grade fit |
|---|---|---|
| Spacecraft clock | 1.9e-12 at 4 h | **RAFS, 100x margin.** CSAC is marginal, do not claim it |
| Ground clock product | 0.334 ns, 3.34 ps/s | Ground rack equipment, outside the space-COTS scope, **and it dominates** |
| Star tracker | 30 arcsec **all axes** | CT-2020 class fits. Small NSTs fail on roll. Two heads is the safe answer |
| Gyro | 0.688 deg/sqrt(h) | **Every** space-grade COTS IMU fits. Not a design driver |
| Receiver | 10 mm carrier, 0.30 m code | Noise is easy. **4 antennas on one clock, tracking ground PN, is not a shelf item** |
| Antenna array | 5 mm PCV, 3.81 mm line bias | Calibration and harness build, not a purchase |
| Structure | not simulated | **Sub-mm CFRP stability on a 2 m baseline is the real attitude driver** |
| Crosslink RF | EIRP 15 dBW, G/T 5 dB/K | 10 cm aperture, under 100 mW. Trivially met |
| Crosslink metrology | **16.7 ps cal, 6.7 ps/h drift** | **No COTS answer.** Purpose-built calibrated transponder |

Three honest conclusions for the write-up:

1. **The simulation is conservative on the components you can buy** (gyro, clock, receiver noise,
   crosslink RF) and silent on the ones you have to engineer (thermoelastic alignment,
   thermoelastic baseline, phase wind-up, picosecond delay calibration).
2. **Two headline numbers in the config are not requirements at all.** ISL EIRP and G/T are inert
   on the branch the goldens run, and the ISL antenna geometry cancels algebraically on the branch
   where a budget is computed.
3. **The two binding requirements are both about time, not about sensors**: the 0.334 ns ground
   clock product and the 16.7 ps crosslink delay calibration.
